**Endpoint and safeguards.** Preserve the implemented nearest-rank premium TTFT p99, pooling request observations over the three blocks **separately for each arm and length**. Never pool lengths, average cell p99s, or import the old v26/calibration observations.

TTFT ends at the gateway’s first-content-forward stamp. Let:

- \(T^+_{A,l}\): pooled scheduled-start p99, uncertain observations assigned \(+\infty\);
- \(T^-_{A,l}\): pooled arrival-start p99, uncertain observations assigned zero;
- \(U_l(A,B)=\ln(T^+_{A,l}/T^-_{B,l})\);
- \(L_l(A,B)=\ln(T^-_{A,l}/T^+_{B,l})=-U_l(B,A)\).

These are conservative measurement bounds, **not confidence intervals**. Recorded failures remain failures as in the existing scorer. An infinite winning numerator cannot establish benefit; two infinities do not order. See [_box and crossed_log_ratio](/home/lkhun9311/workspace/gpu-platform-control-plane/hack/prospective-pilot/pilot_report.py:374).

\[

**My ranked attack on this draft.**

1. **Feasibility remains precarious.** Calibration’s long median cleared 1,740 ms by only about 32 ms. A modest pace change can erase the challenge or break H’s queue limits. Requiring the challenge on every card improves interpretation but makes an unanswered study quite plausible.

2. **Three blocks provide weak replication for p99.** Thousands of requests do not create thousands of independent experimental units. Pooling can hide card-specific reversals, and one seed per card confounds seed variation with card variation. These rules establish observations, not a powered population claim.

3. **The size-aware comparator is calibrated, not optimal.** It transfers one card’s median ratios while the endpoint depends on tails. S can lose because its safety margin transfers poorly; another fixed lookup table could win. Its short spacing is even slightly below calibration’s short prefill p95.

4. **The experiment tests homogeneous workload shifts.** It does not test mixed prompt sizes, transitions during a cell or per-request adaptation under changing mixtures. Calling it general “size-aware robustness” would overstate the evidence.

5. **A win can come from moving or losing work.** The admissibility safeguards permit 10% less shared-window work than off, while directional comparisons permit 3% less than the comparator and one percentage point less completion. Aggregate work equality also permits moving work away from the premium requests that determine p99.

6. **The engine median is an imperfect challenge witness.** Fixed spacing acts at the gateway; transport variation, first-content delivery and rare long prefills determine overlap. Crossing a median threshold does not demonstrate the causal chain. Publish overlap episodes, admission gaps, prefill-tail distributions and premium-tail episodes together.

7. **Order, thermal drift or gateway behaviour can mimic length effects.** Hashed order reduces systematic placement but does not balance every contrast across only three cards. F/S at reference is a valuable identical-treatment check, but cannot fully identify drift.

8. **Cap-only may explain the useful protection.** H could beat an overaggressive fixed timer while adding little beyond the cap. That is why a successful F comparison must retain the cap-only attribution result rather than automatically becoming evidence for feedback.

9. **The result is unusually sensitive to registered conventions.** A single hold refusal defeats admissibility; small p99 rank changes can move a 15% boundary; content-frame gaps are not literal engine-token gaps. Conversely, the 15% non-inferiority margin can label a meaningful slowdown “as well.” Publish raw quantities and boundary distances beside every verdict.
