See [simulate.py:213](/home/lkhun9311/workspace/gpu-platform-control-plane/hack/prospective-pilot/simulate.py:213), [simulate.py:257](/home/lkhun9311/workspace/gpu-platform-control-plane/hack/prospective-pilot/simulate.py:257), and [simulate.py:274](/home/lkhun9311/workspace/gpu-platform-control-plane/hack/prospective-pilot/simulate.py:274).

With no intervening processed notice, the replay refuses it before recovering its legitimate 24.99-second release. A real independent spacing timer could have forwarded it before the deadline. This matters directly to the reported frontier: 1.67 seconds becomes inadmissible through **one hold refusal** in [simulate-feasible.txt:182](/home/lkhun9311/workspace/gpu-platform-control-plane/docs/superpowers/specs/data/2026-10-09-pilot-results/simulate-feasible.txt:182). 2. **High — contender completion can be scored from incomplete timing evidence.**  
   The carried-over scorer reconstructs last-content time as first-token time plus the sum of `contentGapsMicros`. It rejects an absent array, but accepts a truncated or empty array without checking its length: [pilot_report.py:648](/home/lkhun9311/workspace/gpu-platform-control-plane/hack/prospective-pilot/pilot_report.py:648). The corresponding length check exists **only for premium requests**, at [pilot_report.py:208](/home/lkhun9311/workspace/gpu-platform-control-plane/hack/prospective-pilot/pilot_report.py:208).

   Thus a contender reporting complete engine output and `[DONE]`, but missing some recorded gaps, can receive an artificially short completion time. An empty array makes completion equal first-token arrival even when multiple content frames were reported. This can change completion percentage, p50, p95, and therefore which fixed arms are admissible. Contender timing needs the same evidence-completeness check as premium timing.

3. **High — the timing bounds still allow an apparatus delay to masquerade as the signal’s benefit.**  
   Dispatch validity and uncertainty use scheduled-to-gateway-arrival lag, while the gateway performs a synchronous disk write **after** that arrival stamp. The scorer merely reports subsequent delays; it does not bound them. See [requestrecord.go:184](/home/lkhun9311/workspace/gpu-platform-control-plane/internal/gateway/requestrecord.go:184), [requestrecord.go:120](/home/lkhun9311/workspace/gpu-platform-control-plane/internal/gateway/requestrecord.go:120), and [pilot_report.py:224](/home/lkhun9311/workspace/gpu-platform-control-plane/hack/prospective-pilot/pilot_report.py:224).

   Consequently, a delay after arrival can inflate a fixed arm’s premium denominator or move contender work into a different contention pattern while every dispatch check passes. The crossed bounds do not remove it: both scheduled and arrival TTFT include that delay. This was already identified as unresolved at [design:770](/home/lkhun9311/workspace/gpu-platform-control-plane/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-08-measuring-prospective-admission-design.md:770). V26 supplies no new resolution. Its sensitivity to tens of milliseconds makes the missing fidelity check especially relevant.
